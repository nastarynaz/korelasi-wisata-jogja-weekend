# Tourism video narration — five minutes

## 00:00–00:25 — Wisata Jogja di Akhir Pekan

A weekend trip sounds like an obvious reason for an area to become busier. But does the mobility data support that expectation? Our project compares observed daytime device presence in selected Yogyakarta subdistricts that have documented tourist attractions. We compare weekdays and weekends, and ask whether the direction holds across two sample periods.

## 00:25–01:05 — Two sources, seven documented cases

Our mobility source is JogjaPulse from Universitas Gadjah Mada. It reports aggregated device presence by subdistrict and hour. Our second source is the official Visiting Jogja tourism portal. We curated seven documented cases using articles published before December twenty twenty-one, with named administrative locations. These cases include Kalibiru in Kokap and Tebing Breksi in Prambanan. The list is a case study, not a complete inventory. We do not classify the remaining subdistricts as places without tourism.

## 01:05–01:45 — Compare the same hours within each period

We analyze December separately from January through February fifteenth because the provider changed its device sample at the start of January. For each area and date, we calculate mean hourly device presence from eight in the morning through five in the afternoon. All ten hours must be published. Missing slots are never filled with zero. A mean hourly count is not a count of unique daily visitors. For each area and period, weekend uplift is the weekend mean divided by the weekday mean, minus one.

## 01:45–02:25 — Six cases pass the coverage rule

Before ranking areas, we check their data coverage. Each area needs at least ninety percent of the expected hourly slots in both periods. Tanjungsari, the region associated with Pantai Baron, does not meet that rule. It remains visible in the coverage table, but is excluded from the comparison. Six cases remain. Each must also provide at least four complete weekend days and ten complete weekdays in both periods. This selection reduces missing-data problems, but cannot remove bias from application sampling.

## 02:25–03:00 — The weekend pattern is not universal

The bars show the percentage difference between weekend and weekday daytime presence. Kokap is higher on weekends in both periods, by roughly seven percent. Kretek is also higher in both, by about ten percent in December and twenty-one percent in the second period. Pakem, Prambanan, Dlingo, and Cangkringan change direction across periods. These are descriptive comparisons. We have not established that tourism caused the differences, or that every difference is statistically significant.

## 03:00–03:35 — Time of day adds context

The hourly profiles add context to the daytime comparison. We first scale each area by its own average weekday daytime presence within the same sample period. Then we average the regional profiles with equal weights. The green curve represents weekdays and orange represents weekends. Values above one are higher than the weekday daytime baseline. The curves describe all observed devices in selected subdistricts. Residents, local work, and travel unrelated to tourism may all contribute.

## 03:35–04:25 — Different directions across sample periods

This plot compares the December weekend difference with the difference in January through mid-February. Every point represents one selected subdistrict. Kokap and Kretek fall in the positive quadrant because both comparisons are positive. The other four areas change signs. We also repeat the calculation after removing December twenty-fifth, January first, and February first. This is a limited calendar check, not a complete holiday or pandemic adjustment. The short observation window, different device samples, and changing application use limit interpretation. This analysis evaluates presence within an administrative region; it does not observe entry into an attraction or identify individual tourists.

## 04:25–05:00 — A local question, a qualified answer

The answer is mixed. In this selected case study, Kokap and Kretek show higher mean daytime device presence on weekends in both sample periods. Four other areas do not show a consistent direction. The finding is about observed regional activity, rather than destination attendance. Our public data references, notebook, maps, and charts make the calculations inspectable. A longer observation period and direct visitor data would be needed for stronger tourism conclusions. Thank you.
